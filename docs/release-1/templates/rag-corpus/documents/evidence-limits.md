# REPLACE: what this feature's data cannot establish

Replace this whole file. Every corpus needs at least one topic that states what the feature's
evidence does *not* prove, because the rubric marks insufficient-context handling as explicitly as
it marks successful retrieval.

Name the real gaps: coverage that is partial, records that are fixtures rather than live sources,
dates that mean "reviewed on" rather than "published on", and questions a user might reasonably ask
that your sources cannot answer. Being specific here is what lets a grounded answer return a
low or insufficient confidence category with a defensible reason instead of guessing.

Retrieval relevance is not truth. A passage ranking highly means it resembles the question, not
that it is current, authorised or correct. Historical, unrelated and adversarial passages can all
rank highly, so a grounded answer must carry its own support boundary rather than relying on the
retriever having behaved.

Keep this topic honest even where it is unflattering. A documented limitation is markable evidence;
a silent gap that a tutor finds during Q&A is not.
