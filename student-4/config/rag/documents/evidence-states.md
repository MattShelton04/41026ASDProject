# How due-diligence evidence is classified

Every planning, environmental, strata and building record in a review carries an evidence state
and a confidence, so a reader can see not just what was found but how firmly it is established. The
states used are confirmed, partial coverage, non-intersection and unavailable.

## What each evidence state means

Confirmed means the source holds a record that applies to this property, such as a control that
covers the land or a building record on file. Partial coverage means the source applies to the
property only in part, or covers some of the matters you would expect but not all of them.

Non-intersection means a mapped constraint layer, such as a flood or bushfire category, was checked
against the property and did not intersect it. Unavailable means the source could not be reached or
holds no record, so nothing can be concluded either way. Confidence describes how sure the source
is about its own record; it is not a statement of legal or structural certainty.

## Reading a due-diligence summary honestly

Read confirmed evidence alongside what is unavailable or only partially covered. A review that
looks reassuring because little was flagged may simply have many unavailable sources, and those
gaps are the priority to verify, not a clean result.

For that reason the assistant prioritises questions about unavailable and partial-coverage evidence
when it builds a verification pack. A non-intersection result reduces concern about that specific
hazard layer but does not close the matter, and it says nothing about controls or building records
held by other sources.
