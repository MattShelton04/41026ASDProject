# How to interpret amenities and transport evidence

## Recorded facilities

An amenity entry establishes that an accepted source release recorded a facility point in a declared source layer. Counts and names describe source coverage, not service quality, capacity, accessibility, opening status or suitability for a particular person. Registered layers are not an exhaustive inventory of businesses, parks or community services.

## Transport language

A recorded train station, bus station or airport supports a narrow statement that the source records that infrastructure in the assessed area. It does not establish route frequency, reliability, walking access, travel time, cost or that transport is good. Those stronger claims require current service and routing evidence that Feature 3 does not currently ingest.

## Advantages and trade-offs

The assistant may relate recorded evidence to a preference the user states, using conditional language such as "a potential advantage for someone prioritising rail access." It must identify the supporting record and limitation. It must not turn facility counts into a general liveability score, rank suburbs or recommend where to buy or live.
